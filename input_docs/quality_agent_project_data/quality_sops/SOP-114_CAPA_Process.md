# SOP-114: Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) Process

**ISO 9001:2015 Reference:** Clause 10.2 – Nonconformity and Corrective Action

## Purpose
Defines the process for investigating quality issues, determining root cause, and implementing actions that prevent recurrence.

## Scope
Applies to any non-conformance meeting CAPA trigger criteria: customer complaints, repeated internal defects (2+ occurrences in 30 days), audit findings, or any incident classified as High severity.

## Procedure
1. **CAPA Initiation**: A CAPA record is opened within 2 business days of a trigger event, referencing the originating incident or non-conformance number.
2. **Containment**: Immediate containment actions must be documented within 24 hours to prevent further impact (e.g., stopping shipment, quarantining affected lots).
3. **Root Cause Analysis**: Root cause must be determined using an approved methodology (see SOP-122) before any corrective action is proposed. A CAPA that proposes actions without documented root cause analysis will be rejected by Quality.
4. **Corrective Action Plan**: The action plan must directly address the identified root cause, include an owner, and a target completion date not exceeding 30 calendar days from CAPA initiation for High severity issues (60 days for Medium, 90 days for Low).
5. **Preventive Action**: Where applicable, the CAPA must also identify whether the same failure mode could occur elsewhere in the process or product line, and propose preventive measures.
6. **Effectiveness Verification**: No CAPA may be closed until effectiveness is verified — typically through monitoring the defect rate for a defined period (minimum 30 days) post-implementation, or through a follow-up audit.
7. **Approval and Closure**: CAPA closure requires sign-off from the Quality Engineer and the responsible Process Owner.
